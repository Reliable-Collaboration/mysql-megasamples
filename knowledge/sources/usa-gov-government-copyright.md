# What was read
USA.gov's page on copyright in U.S. government works, read 2026-09-02.

# Relevant excerpt
> "Government work is something created by a U.S. government officer or employee as part of their official duties."

U.S. government works are generally not subject to copyright protection. The page lists the exceptions that survive:
> "Content on federal websites may include protected intellectual property used with the right holder's permission."
> "You also cannot use federal government trademarks or federal government agency logos without permission."
> "You cannot use government materials in a way that implies endorsement by a government agency, official, or employee."
> "U.S. copyright laws may not protect U.S. government works outside the country."

It advises checking with the agency that manages the site before assuming material is unrestricted.
